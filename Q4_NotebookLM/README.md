# Q4 — Google NotebookLM Evaluation for ESG Research

## Project Overview

For Question 4 of my assessment, I tested Google NotebookLM to evaluate how useful it is for academic and ESG-related research.

I used two United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) reports:

- Emissions Gap Report 2023
- Emissions Gap Report 2024

My aim was to examine NotebookLM's ability to summarise information, provide source citations, generate Mind Maps, handle missing information and compare findings across documents.

## Experiments Conducted

### Test 1: Summarisation and Citation Verification

I uploaded the 2024 report and asked NotebookLM to identify three important findings about global greenhouse gas emissions and climate targets.

NotebookLM produced a structured summary with numerical evidence and citations.

I checked one of its claims against the original report. The figure of 57.1 GtCO2e for global emissions in 2023 and the 1.3% increase from 2022 were supported by the cited passage.

### Test 2: Mind Map

I generated a Mind Map using the 2024 report.

NotebookLM organised the content into six categories, including global emissions, climate targets, emissions gaps, mitigation solutions and investment requirements.

I found this feature useful for exploring the structure of a long report, although some branches provided limited explanation.

### Test 3: Missing Information

I asked NotebookLM for the actual global greenhouse gas emissions figure for the full year 2025 using the 2024 report.

The tool correctly explained that this information was unavailable in the uploaded source.

This demonstrated that NotebookLM could acknowledge missing information in this particular test.

### Test 4: Cross-Source Comparison

I uploaded the 2023 report and asked NotebookLM to compare findings across both documents.

NotebookLM generated a comparison table covering emissions, warming projections, climate targets and mitigation actions.

I also investigated the different historical 2022 emissions estimates reported in the two editions. NotebookLM identified updated historical datasets and more detailed methane emissions information as the explanation.

## Evaluation

I found NotebookLM useful for quickly exploring lengthy ESG documents and locating relevant information.

Its main strengths were structured summaries, source citations, visual organisation and cross-document comparisons.

However, I also identified limitations. Citations need to be checked, Mind Maps may lack detailed explanations, and numerical comparisons can be misleading if reporting methodologies change.

I would use NotebookLM as a research assistant rather than relying on its answers without verification.
